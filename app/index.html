What Starling is and how it works ↗

How it works

  1. 1

    Create a circle

    No email, no phone number, nothing to sign. The app generates your keys on your device and never sends them anywhere.

  2. 2

    Send one link

    The invite burns after one use. Before it opens, you and your person read a short safety number out loud to each other and check it matches.

  3. 3

    Watch each other arrive

    Every position is sealed on your phone and opened on theirs. The relay in the middle moves envelopes it cannot open.

Private by construction, not by promise

End-to-end encrypted

Every position is sealed with AES-256-GCM on your device. The relay moves ciphertext it cannot read.

Forward secrecy

Keys retire every ten minutes. A relay seizure yields at most one day of ciphertext, and less for any epoch already passed.

Places stay on your phone

Name a Home, School, or Work and the app tells you who arrived and who left. The server never learns those places exist.

SOS and help beacon

Hold to send SOS to your circle, or open a revocable beacon link that works in any browser. Members also get a heads up when someone's battery is about to die.

Everything expires

The relay deletes every row after 24 hours and keeps no backups, so there is no history sitting around to leak.

Lock it, or torch it

An optional passcode and biometric lock encrypt your circle secret at rest. Panic wipe erases the lot instantly, and a duress passcode does it silently if you're ever forced to unlock.

Off-grid and Orbot

Switch the basemap off and the app makes zero requests beyond the relay. Route it through Orbot and the relay only ever sees Tor.

Circles, plural

One for family, one for the friends you split up from at a fair. Separate keys, separate identities, one tap to switch.

Yours to verify

MIT licensed, reproducible Android builds, an open protocol, and a relay you can self-host.

What the relay can see

This is the part most apps do not put on their homepage. Here is the honest line between what a full seizure of the relay's database would get someone, and what it would not.

Never

  • Positions. Every location is AES-256-GCM encrypted on your device with a key that never leaves it.
  • Names, avatars, or circle names. Identity travels only inside ciphertext.
  • Accounts, phone numbers, emails, or push tokens. There is nothing to link a channel to a person except traffic metadata.
  • History past 24 hours. Rows expire on schedule and there are no backups.

Learns, honestly

  • IP addresses and timing. Every poll and post reveals a source IP and a cadence.
  • How many members post to a channel. Not what they say: every message pads to the same length.
  • A correlation signal between an SOS and its circle. The keys are unlinkable; the timing is not.
  • That you use Starling at all, from the origin your device talks to.

Common questions

Is it free?

Yes. No ads, no premium tier, nothing to unlock. It is MIT licensed, so you can also read every line that touches your position.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. Android is where the app lives today, but the protocol is open, so an iOS client is possible. The website itself does not open circles on purpose: a browser tab is the weakest place to keep a location secret.

How is this different from mainstream family trackers?

Their servers see every position you ever send. Starling's relay cannot see any of them, because they never leave your device unencrypted. There is nothing on the relay worth selling or worth a breach.

What if someone grabs my invite link?

Invites are one-time. Opening one starts a safety number check, so the person already in the circle can see it is not who they expected. Anyone you no longer trust can be removed with a re-key that cuts them off for good.

What does this do to my battery?

Sharing runs as a foreground service so the OS does not kill it, which does cost some battery. It is the same trade-off any always-on location app makes, and sharing is off until you turn it on.

Can I run my own relay?

Yes. The relay is a small Cloudflare Worker and the setup is documented. Point the app at your own instance and Munzzyy's relay never sees your traffic at all. See the protocol docs for how.

What happens in an emergency?

Hold the SOS button and your circle gets an alert at your live position. On their phones it lands as a notification while Starling runs in the background; a phone that is off or has the app fully closed sees it on the next open, because there is no push service to force it awake. You can also open a help beacon, a link that works in any browser, for someone outside your circle, and revoke it the moment you are safe.

What does Starling not do?

No iPhone app yet. No driving reports, speed alerts, or crash detection. No location history older than a day, on purpose. If those are the features you need most, Starling is not your app yet, and finding that out here beats finding it out after you switched.
